Rota O daily Tao

Thee Turning Rota

One chapter of the Tao Te Ching for each day, time written in Cistercian numerals, and the sky as one slowly turning wheel. Rota is the Latin word for wheel; RoTao is the wheel and the Way.

Touch the yin-yang to unfold today’s chapter.

The chapter changes at 00:00 UTC. Counting from 1 January 2026 it moves through all 81 chapters and begins again, so the same chapter returns every 81 days.

Time, in Cistercian numerals

In the thirteenth century Cistercian monks wrote any number from 1 to 9999 as a single sign: a vertical stem with a digit hung on each of its four corners. Here the hours, minutes and seconds each get a sign, and so does the date.

Simulated time

How to read the signs

The stem is the zero. Four corners hold four digits: units upper right, tens upper left, hundreds lower right and thousands lower left. Every corner uses the same nine shapes, turned over or mirrored for the other corners. A number is read by adding the corners, so a single sign can carry anything from 0 to 9999.

The celestial clock

A chart wheel with the Earth in its middle. The map is seen from above the North Pole, with the night side in shadow and the Sun and the Moon over the places where they stand at zenith. Around it turn the zodiac and the planets. Press play, speed it up, or jump to any date.

The celestial clock loads as you scroll to it.

The Moon calendar

The Moon, month by month: her phase for every day, the sign she stands in, the exact time of each New, First Quarter, Full and Last Quarter Moon, and of each aspect she makes to the Sun and the planets. Choose your own time or UTC, then save the month as a picture.

The Moon calendar loads as you scroll to it.

The Tree of Life

Ten spheres and the twenty-two paths between them, from Kether, the Crown, down to Malkuth, the Kingdom. It stays here, whatever the hour.

The Gate of Splendor

Zohar means “splendor”. The title page of the book is a gate, and over it is written zeh ha-sha’ar, “this is the gate” (Psalm 118:20). Touch it and a passage of the Zohar opens, in its own Aramaic and in English, a new one each day.

The Torah, a reading a day

The Torah is sung of as a tree of life to those who hold fast to it (Proverbs 3:18). Touch the tree and the portion of the week opens, divided as it is read in the synagogue: the 1st reading on Sunday, the 2nd on Monday, and so on to the 7th and the maftir on Shabbat. The Hebrew is the Leningrad Codex with its vowels and chant marks, the English is the Jewish Publication Society’s translation of 1917.
